Treat Energy Availability as a Service Requirement

A short synopsis of M. Murali’s Energy Sovereignty: Rethinking Range Anxiety in the Electric Vehicle with an operating implication for enterprise leaders.

By NetworkGain Editorial

NetworkGain Point of View

A short synopsis of M. Murali’s Energy Sovereignty: Rethinking Range Anxiety in the Electric Vehicle with an operating implication for enterprise leaders.

Source: Energy Sovereignty: Rethinking Range Anxiety in the Electric Vehicle, by M. Murali, published 2026-09-22. This is an independently written synopsis; the operating implication is NetworkGain’s editorial interpretation.

M. Murali reframes range anxiety as uncertainty about obtaining energy during a journey. His energy-sovereignty proposal considers multiple pathways, including onboard generation, temporary external modules and mobile energy services, alongside the primary charging network.

The article’s underlying requirement is journey completion. Battery capacity is one contributor to that outcome; infrastructure reliability and contingency arrangements also matter. The proposed alternatives remain architectures to evaluate, with their own engineering, operating and environmental trade-offs. They should not be read as recommendations to modify an existing vehicle.

The NetworkGain operating implication

evaluate fleet energy as an end-to-end service. Map route demand, charging availability, downtime and recovery arrangements. Compare contingency options against the actual frequency and cost of disrupted journeys. Select an assurance model only after validating compatibility and full lifecycle economics.
