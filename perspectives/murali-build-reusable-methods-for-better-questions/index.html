Build Reusable Methods for Better Questions

A short synopsis of M. Murali’s From Prompt Engineering to Epistemic Generativity with an operating implication for enterprise leaders.

By NetworkGain Editorial

NetworkGain Point of View

A short synopsis of M. Murali’s From Prompt Engineering to Epistemic Generativity with an operating implication for enterprise leaders.

Source: From Prompt Engineering to Epistemic Generativity, by M. Murali, published 2026-08-11. This is an independently written synopsis; the operating implication is NetworkGain’s editorial interpretation.

M. Murali proposes treating prompts as operations on knowledge. Comparing, decomposing, challenging assumptions and transferring a principle between domains become reusable methods for inquiry. The ambition extends from obtaining a useful response to producing better questions and further avenues of investigation.

He describes compact generative ideas as epistemic seeds: structures that can support repeated exploration across contexts. This is a conceptual framework, with mathematical growth language used metaphorically. More generated material does not, by itself, establish more reliable knowledge.

The NetworkGain operating implication

organise a small inquiry library by thinking operation and evidence requirement. For each method, retain an example, its limitations and a decision it improved. Evaluate whether it exposes assumptions or produces testable alternatives, rather than measuring success through prompt counts or the volume of AI output.
